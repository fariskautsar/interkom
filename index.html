01

Questions & Method

Students use YouTube every day for both studying and relaxing, while ads and Shorts compete for their attention. We asked where the app gets in the way of its users.

Research questions

  1. What difficulties do active users face, and how often?
  2. How usable and satisfying is the app, based on the SUS?
  3. Where do users fail, slow down or make errors in common tasks?
  4. What design changes do users prefer?

Why mixed methods?

The survey shows how common a problem is. The interviews explain why it happens. The observation shows what people actually do, not only what they say. When two or more methods agree, the finding gets a higher priority.

01

Interviews

5 participants, semi-structured guide of 16 questions, analysed with thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006).

02

Survey

Google Forms, 30 responses. SUS, NPS, satisfaction per feature, difficulties and design preferences.

03

Observation

5 participants (3 phone, 2 laptop) did 7 tasks on their own devices while thinking aloud.

04

Triangulation

Findings compared across methods. A problem found by 2+ methods gets a higher priority.

Users are loyal and satisfied, but the interface itself is only average to use.

Only 9 of 20 respondents scored above the benchmark of 68 (Lewis, 2018). On the adjective scale (Bangor et al., 2008), 66.8 is “OK”.

Net Promoter Score: +50

“How likely are you to recommend YouTube?” 0–10, n = 24

Promoters 9–10: 16 (67%) Passives 7–8: 4 (17%) Detractors 0–6: 4 (17%)

NPS = % promoters − % detractors = 67 − 17 = +50. Overall satisfaction is 4.17 / 5. Yet SUS and satisfaction are not correlated (Spearman ρ = −0.05, p = 0.82): users stay for the content, not the interface.

05

Interviews & Observation

All 5 interviewees mentioned ads, and both Premium users pay mainly to avoid them. In the observation, everyone completed every task.

P1 · Free · iPad

“I think there would be a study mode … the recommendations will only recommend you study materials.”

P2 · Premium · Phone

“A one-minute ad that you can’t skip. A year ago, it wasn’t like this.”

P3 · Free · Phone

“I wish it had a proper Study Mode with no Shorts and no distractions.”

P4 · Premium · Multi-device

Without Premium, “ads can appear up to 3 times and cannot be skipped.”

P5 · Free · Phone

“It’s free content, so I expect ads.” Shorts are “the ultimate time-killer”.

Also raised

Free background play and downloads (P3, P4, P5), messy watch history (P3), music queue control (P2), sharing videos (P1).

07

Design Recommendations

Ordered by priority. Each mockup shows the current app (simplified) next to the proposed design. Click a mockup to enlarge it.

R1 High priority

Clearer, more predictable ads

Keep the ads, but make them predictable and easier to skip.

  • Markers on the progress bar show where ad breaks are
  • 5-second countdown before an ad, with the break length
  • Option to watch the ads before a long video instead of in the middle
  • Larger Skip button, away from other controls
  • Clear ad label with the time remaining

Evidence: most common difficulty (62.5%), lowest-rated feature (3.08 / 5), raised by 50% in open answers and by all 5 interviewees. Heuristics: visibility of system status; user control and freedom.

R4 High priority

Focus and study mode

An optional mode that removes distractions while studying.

  • Banner shows Focus mode is on, with one-tap Turn off
  • Shorts and recommendations hidden, with “Show anyway”
  • Starts automatically for a video the user searched for
  • Daily Shorts reminder with an adjustable limit

Evidence: 83% use YouTube to learn, 58% want a focus mode, 25% open Shorts by accident, asked for by P1 and P3. Heuristics: user control and freedom; aesthetic and minimalist design.

R3 Medium priority

Recommendation control

Make it faster to tell YouTube what you don’t want to see.

  • Swipe or long-press a card for “Not interested” or “Don’t recommend channel”
  • Confirmation says what will change, with Undo
  • “Tune my feed” panel: each topic set to Less, Normal or More

Evidence: 46% report irrelevant recommendations, 67% want more control, 1 phone user hesitated on T5. Heuristics: user control and freedom; recognition rather than recall.

R2 Medium priority

Organized saved content

One searchable place for saved videos, playlists and history.

  • One search bar across saved videos, playlists and history
  • Tag chips such as Study, Music and Exam
  • Sort by topic or by date
  • Folders to group saved videos by purpose

Evidence: most wanted change in the survey (75%), 25% find saved videos hard to find; only partly confirmed by interviews (P3) and not by the observation. Heuristics: recognition rather than recall; flexibility and efficiency of use.

08 · Conclusion

What we found

  1. 01Ads are the number one problem: reported by 62.5%, the lowest-rated feature (3.08 / 5), and mentioned by all 5 interviewees.
  2. 02Usability is only average (SUS 66.8, below the benchmark of 68), yet users are loyal (NPS +50, satisfaction 4.17 / 5). They stay for the content, not the interface.
  3. 03Core tasks are easy: all 5 observed participants completed all 7 tasks. What users want is more control over ads, recommendations, focus and saved content.

Limitations: convenience sample (24 valid responses, mostly aged 18–24), 5 interviews, small Premium group (5–6), so results are descriptive. Future work: a larger and more varied sample, and usability or A/B testing of the proposed mockups.
